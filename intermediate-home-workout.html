4-day home workout: an upper/lower dumbbell plan

A 4-day upper/lower plan with dumbbells, a bench and a pull-up bar: a gentler start if you're new, and how to keep progressing at home once you're not.

By Volodymyr and Linus4 min readUpdated November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Four days a week: two upper-body and two lower-body sessions, about 10–15 hard sets per muscle a week.
  • New? Do 2 sets instead of 3 for the first two weeks. Experienced? Take most sets to 0–2 reps from failure.
  • Out of weight? Use single-limb exercises, slow reps, pauses and higher rep ranges before buying heavier dumbbells.

If you can train four days a week at home, this plan splits your week into upper-body and lower-body days, so each muscle gets more work and more recovery. It works for beginners and for people who've trained for years; only how hard you push changes.

Who it's for

  • You can train 4 days a week.
  • You have dumbbells (adjustable ones are ideal, up to 24 kg / 50 lb or more each as you get stronger).
  • A bench and a pull-up bar help. No bench? Do the presses on the floor. No pull-up bar? Swap pull-ups and chin-ups for one-arm rows.
  • New to training? Read the gentler start first.

Dumbbells are the main equipment for this plan; choose a weight range that suits your current lifts and leaves room to progress. The adjustable bench supports the incline exercises, and the pull-up bar is optional. Check doorway compatibility before buying.

New to training? Start like this

  1. Weeks 1–2: do 2 sets instead of 3 on every exercise, and stop each set when you could still do 2–3 more good reps.
  2. Use the easier swaps: band-assisted pull-ups or one-arm rows instead of pull-ups, regular push-ups instead of feet-raised ones, goblet squats instead of Bulgarian split squats.
  3. From week 3: move to the full sets and train a bit closer to failure.

If four days turns out to be too much, the 3-day dumbbell plan works just as well. Showing up every week matters more than the number of days.

How hard and how to progress

  • Experienced: take most sets to 0–2 reps from failure. Beginners: stay about 1–3 reps away while you learn the movements.
  • Use double progression: reach the top of the rep range on every set, then go up in weight.
  • Track every session. At this stage progress is slower, and your log is the only way to see it.
  • If you feel run down for a couple of weeks, do one lighter week with fewer sets, then carry on.

Cool-down (5 minutes)

After your last exercise, walk or cycle easily for 2–3 minutes, then do 3–4 gentle stretches for the muscles you trained, 20–30 seconds each. See the 5-minute cool-down routine for which stretches go with which day.

When you run out of weight

Heavy lower-body work is the hardest part of training at home. Before buying heavier dumbbells:

  1. Use single-leg versions: Bulgarian split squats, single-leg hip thrusts and single-leg Romanian deadlifts double the load per leg.
  2. Slow the lowering part to 3 seconds and pause at the bottom.
  3. Work in higher rep ranges (up to about 20–30), as long as sets end close to failure.

Key insight

At this stage, sleep, protein and consistency matter more than finding a better exercise. If progress stalls, check those first; our recovery guide explains how.
