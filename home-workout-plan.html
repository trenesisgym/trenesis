Home workouts: pick the right plan for you

Four home workout plans for every situation: no equipment, a pair of dumbbells, short sessions for busy weeks and a 4-day plan. Plus the rules that make home training work.

By Volodymyr and Linus2 min readUpdated October 2026

Key takeaways

  • You can build muscle at home; the rules are the same as in a gym.
  • Pick a plan by your equipment and experience: bodyweight, dumbbells, 20-minute sessions or a 4-day split.
  • Keep making it harder: more reps, harder variations, slower reps or one side at a time.

You can build muscle and strength at home. The rules are the same as in a gym: train each muscle a few times a week, take sets close to failure, and make them harder over time. What changes is how you make them harder.

The rules that make home training work

  1. Train each muscle 2–3 times a week. All four plans do this for you.
  2. Finish sets close to failure. Stop when you could do about 1–2 more good reps.
  3. Write everything down. Exercise, level or weight, and reps. Beat it a little next time.

How to keep progressing without heavier weights

When an exercise gets easy, make it harder in one of these ways:

  1. Add reps. Once you can do 15–20 good reps, move to a harder version.
  2. Use a harder variation. Hands-raised push-ups, then floor push-ups, then feet-raised push-ups. Squats, then split squats, then Bulgarian split squats.
  3. Slow down. Take 3 seconds on the way down; the same exercise becomes much harder.
  4. Train one side at a time. Single-leg and single-arm versions double the load on each side.

Key insight

Your muscles don't know whether the resistance is a machine, a dumbbell or your bodyweight. What matters is that each set ends close to failure and that it gets harder over time.

When to add equipment

A pull-up bar and a pair of adjustable dumbbells cover most of what you'll need for a long time. Our equipment guide explains what to look for and shows picks for your country.
