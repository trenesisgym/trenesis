Cardio

3 cardio plans for beginners and lifters (with sample weeks)

Three ready-made weekly cardio plans, from a 3-day base builder to a 5-day performance week, plus common mistakes and whether cardio kills muscle gains.

3 min read · Updated October 2026

Pick the plan that fits your schedule and current fitness, then stick with it for at least 8–12 weeks. Not sure what "Zone 2" means? Read heart rate zones explained first.

Plan A: 3-day aerobic base builder

Three Zone 2 sessions a week. Ideal for beginners or anyone coming back after a break, and it fits well alongside a strength program. Use any low-impact option you enjoy: walking, cycling, swimming or easy running.

Day Session
Monday 30 min Zone 2
Wednesday 30 min Zone 2
Friday 35 min Zone 2

Plan B: 4-day endurance build

Three base sessions plus one tempo or threshold session. It speeds up your aerobic progress while adding some controlled hard work.

Day Session
Monday 35 min Zone 2
Wednesday 25 min intervals: 4 × 4 min hard (Zone 4), 3 min easy
Friday 40 min Zone 2
Sunday 45 min Zone 2, long session

Plan C: 5-day performance week

For people with a solid base. Four easy days and one hard interval day, following the 80/20 rule. Good for improving a 5K or 10K time, or for strong conditioning alongside lifting.

Day Session
Monday 40 min Zone 2
Tuesday 30 min Zone 2
Wednesday HIIT: 6 × 2 min hard (Zone 5), 2 min walk
Friday 45 min Zone 2
Saturday 50 min Zone 2, long run

Common mistakes

  • Living in the grey zone. Most sessions should feel genuinely easy; keep the hard ones hard.
  • Not tracking. Log duration, distance and heart rate. Weekly data shows progress you can't feel day to day.
  • Skipping the warm-up. Spend 5–10 minutes easy (Zone 1) before any moderate or hard session.
  • Under-fuelling. Sessions over an hour need food and fluids; chronic under-eating leads to fatigue and muscle loss.
  • Only doing cardio. Without resistance training you lose muscle over time, especially while dieting. Do at least two strength sessions a week.
  • Program hopping. Cardio fitness takes weeks to build. Give a plan 8–12 weeks before judging it.

Questions everyone asks

Will cardio kill my muscle gains?

Not if you program it sensibly. Lots of hard cardio on top of a full lifting program can interfere with recovery, but 3–4 Zone 2 sessions a week won't meaningfully affect muscle growth, and they'll improve your work capacity in the gym.

Cardio before or after lifting?

For most people, lift first: being tired from cardio hurts strength work more than the other way round. If endurance is your main goal, flip it, or ideally separate them by 6+ hours or do them on different days.

How long until I see results?

Your heart and blood volume adapt within 2–4 weeks. Noticeable VO2 max gains usually take 6–8 weeks; body composition changes from cardio alone take 8–16 weeks.

Is soreness a sign of a good session?

Not really. Mild leg soreness after starting running is normal, but the real sign of progress is your heart rate at the same pace dropping over the weeks.

Sources
