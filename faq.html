Getting started

I’ve never trained. Where do I start?

Take the 4-question plan finder. It picks a plan for where you train and how many days you have. If you’re heading to a gym, read your first week at the gym first. Most beginners end up on the 3-day full-body plan.

How long until I see results?

Most beginners get noticeably stronger within 2–4 weeks, mainly because your body learns the movements. Visible muscle usually takes 2–3 months of consistent training and enough protein. Fat loss shows on the scale within a few weeks if you eat in a small deficit, about 0.5–1% of your bodyweight a week. Expect weekly ups and downs: judge the trend over a month, not a single day.

Gym or home: which is better?

Both work. A gym makes it easier to keep adding weight, which is what drives progress. At home, a pair of adjustable dumbbells gets you surprisingly far, and the bodyweight plan works with no equipment at all. The best option is the one you’ll stick with. Compare the home plans if you’re unsure.

How many days a week should I train?

Three full-body workouts a week is plenty to start. Four works well too if you’ll actually show up four times, and we have 4-day plans with a gentler start for beginners. Training every muscle about twice a week matters more than the exact number of days.

Are the plans for women too?

Yes. Muscle grows the same way in every body, so the same principles and plans apply. You don’t need a separate “toning” plan: toning is building some muscle and, if you want, losing some fat. Many women like to add extra glute work, and the plans already include hip thrusts. The calculator adjusts calories for sex, age and height.

Will lifting make me bulky?

No. Building muscle is slow, even when you try hard to gain it. You stay in control: how much you eat decides whether you get bigger, stay the same size or get leaner.

I’m over 40 (or 60). Is it too late to start?

Not at all. Strength training helps at every age: for strength, bone health, balance and staying independent. Start with lighter weights, warm up a bit longer and stay 2–3 reps away from failure for the first weeks. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure or joint problems, check with your doctor first.

I’m a teenager. Can I lift weights?

Yes. With good technique and sensible weights, strength training is considered safe and useful for young people (NSCA position statement). Learn the movements with light weights first, ideally with a coach or experienced adult around. Skip dieting: your body needs enough food to grow, which is why our calculator doesn’t give a fat-loss target under 18.

Do I need to be sore after a workout for it to work?

No. Soreness mostly means you did something new. It fades as your body adapts, while your progress continues. Track your weights and reps instead: if they go up over the weeks, it’s working.

Training

How heavy should I lift?

Pick a weight you can lift for the bottom of the rep range with good form, and stop each set when you could still do 1–2 more good reps. In your first two weeks, stay a bit further from that point while you learn. When you hit the top of the range on every set, add the smallest weight step. The double progression rule explains it step by step.

What if a machine is busy, or my gym doesn’t have it?

Swap it for another exercise for the same muscle, with similar sets and reps. Our exercise list per muscle gives options for each one. Use the same swap every time so you can track your progress.

How long should a workout take?

About 45–60 minutes, including warm-up sets and rest. Short on time? The 20-minute workouts keep your progress going in busy weeks.

Do I need to cool down after a workout?

It’s a good habit, not a must. A cool-down brings your heart rate and breathing back down and is a convenient moment to stretch, but research shows it doesn’t prevent soreness or speed up recovery much. Five minutes is plenty: see our 5-minute cool-down routine.

Should I do cardio as well?

Yes, for your heart and your recovery. Two or three easy sessions a week, like brisk walking, cycling or a light jog, are plenty and won’t hurt your strength gains. See our cardio plans.

I missed a workout (or a week). What now?

Carry on with the next workout in the order. One missed session changes nothing. After a week or more off, use slightly lighter weights for your first session back.

When should I change my plan?

Not as long as you’re still adding reps or weight. Change something when you stop progressing for 3 weeks or more even though you sleep and eat well, usually after a few months. How to build a workout program shows what to adjust.

Something hurts. Should I train through it?

Muscle soreness a day or two after training is normal. Sharp pain, pain in a joint, or pain that gets worse during a set is not: stop that exercise and try a pain-free swap. If it doesn’t settle within a week or two, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Nutrition

Do I have to count calories?

No, but tracking for 2–3 weeks teaches you a lot about portions. After that, many people can eat by habit and check the scale. Get your starting numbers from the calorie and protein calculator.

How much protein do I need?

About 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight a day (0.7–1 g per lb). If you carry a lot of extra weight, base it on a healthy weight for your height. More in how much protein you need.

Do I need supplements?

No. Food, sleep and consistent training do the work. Protein powder is a convenient way to hit your protein target, and creatine is the one performance supplement with strong evidence. Everything else is optional at best. See supplements that actually work.

Can I build muscle and lose fat at the same time?

Often, yes, especially as a beginner or after a break. Eat around maintenance or in a small deficit, keep protein high and train hard. Progress is slower than focusing on one goal, but it works.

How fast should I lose weight?

About 0.5–1% of your bodyweight a week. Faster usually costs muscle, energy and motivation. Our fat loss meal tips help you stay full.

I’m vegetarian or vegan. Does that change anything?

Not much. Aim for the same protein target from foods like tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans, soy milk and plant protein powder. Our meal guides include plant-based example days.

How does the workout logbook work?

Pick your plan in the logbook, enter your weight and reps after each set and press Save. Next time it shows what you did last time, so you know what to beat. You can swap any exercise, add sets or extra exercises, or build your own plan, for example Push/Pull/Legs. It saves on your device only: nothing is sent to us. Download a backup now and then to keep your log safe or move it to another device.

How accurate is the calorie calculator?

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most accurate everyday formula for healthy adults, but any formula can be off by around 10% for a given person. Eat that way for 2–3 weeks, watch your average weight and adjust by 100–200 kcal. That real-world check beats any formula.
