Carbs and fats: how to set up your macros in 4 steps

What carbohydrates and fats do for training and health, a simple 4-step order for setting your macros, and straight answers on carbs, soy and rest-day eating.

3 min readUpdated October 2026

Carbs and fat both have a job, and neither is the enemy. Once you hit your calorie and protein targets, how you split the rest is largely about preference and what keeps you feeling good.

Carbohydrates: training fuel

Carbs are your body's preferred fuel for hard exercise. Stored carbohydrate in your muscles (glycogen) powers your sets. When it runs low, performance and training volume drop. Eating carbs a few hours before training can help you perform better.

Fat: essential, but calorie-dense

Dietary fat is needed for hormone production (including testosterone), absorbing vitamins A, D, E and K, and healthy cells. It has 9 kcal per gram versus 4 kcal for protein and carbs, so portions add up quickly in a deficit.

Favour unsaturated fats (olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds and fatty fish) and keep saturated fat (fatty meat, butter, full-fat dairy) moderate.

Setting up your macros: 4 steps

Step What to do
1. Calories Set them for your goal: deficit, maintenance or surplus (how to).
2. Protein 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight per day (why).
3. Carbs At least 3–5 g per kg of bodyweight if you train regularly.
4. Fat Fill the remaining calories; usually about 0.6–1 g per kg of bodyweight.

Questions everyone asks

Are carbs bad for you?

No. Quality matters (vegetables, whole grains and fruit beat ultra-processed snacks), but carbs aren't inherently fattening. Eating more calories than you burn causes fat gain, whatever they come from.

Does soy lower testosterone?

No. Soy contains phytoestrogens, which resemble oestrogen in structure but don't act like it at normal intakes. Meta-analyses find no meaningful effect of soy on men's testosterone levels.

Should I eat less on rest days?

Slightly less is fine, but big cuts aren't necessary. A lot of muscle repair happens on rest days, so you still need enough protein and calories.

Do I need to count calories?

Not necessarily, but awareness matters. If you aren't getting results, tracking for 2–4 weeks gives you a baseline. Most people underestimate how much they eat until they measure it.

How important is meal timing?

Much less than your total daily intake. Hit your calories, spread protein over 3–5 meals, and whether you eat breakfast or not is up to you.

Can I build muscle as a vegetarian or vegan?

Yes. Plan your protein: plant sources are often less protein-dense, so combine several (tofu, tempeh, seitan, legumes) and use a plant protein powder if needed.

Sources

  • Reed KE et al. Neither soy nor isoflavone intake affects male reproductive hormones: an expanded and updated meta-analysis of clinical studies. Reproductive Toxicology, 2021.
