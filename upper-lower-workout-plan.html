4-day upper/lower gym plan: a free program for beginners and beyond

A complete 4-day gym plan: two upper-body and two lower-body workouts a week, with sets, reps, rest times, a gentler start for beginners and a simple rule for adding weight.

By Volodymyr and Linus4 min readUpdated November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Four workouts a week: Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, Lower B, about 45–60 minutes each.
  • New to the gym? Do 2 sets instead of 3 for the first two weeks and stay 2–3 reps from failure.
  • Add weight once you hit the top of the rep range on every set, and write everything down.

If you can train four days a week, an upper/lower split is one of the simplest ways to use them. You train your upper body twice and your lower body twice, so every muscle gets worked twice a week with enough rest in between. That works whether it's your first month in the gym or your second year.

The week

Monday Tuesday Wednesday Thursday Friday Weekend
Upper A Lower A Rest Upper B Lower B Rest

Any four days work. Try not to train more than two days in a row, and keep the order: Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, Lower B. Each workout takes about 45–60 minutes.

New to the gym? Start like this

You don't need months of experience for this plan. Just ease in:

  1. Weeks 1–2: do 2 sets instead of 3 on every exercise, and stop each set when you could still do 2–3 more good reps.
  2. Pick the machine option (machine chest press, leg press) while you learn the movements. Free weights can come later.
  3. From week 3: move to the full sets and train a bit closer to failure.

Read your first week at the gym before your first session. If four days turns out to be too much, the 3-day full-body plan works just as well. Showing up every week matters more than the number of days.

How hard each set should be

Stop each set when you could do about 1–2 more good reps. Always do 1–2 lighter warm-up sets before the first exercise of the day.

How to progress: the double progression rule

  1. Pick a weight you can lift for the bottom of the rep range, for example 8 reps.
  2. Each session, try to add a rep or two with the same weight.
  3. Once you hit the top of the range on every set, add the smallest weight step available.
  4. Your reps drop back to the bottom of the range. Build them up again and repeat.

Write down every set. That log is how you know the plan is working.

Key insight

Training a muscle twice a week builds more muscle than once a week. Whether you get there with three full-body days or four upper/lower days matters much less. Pick the plan you'll actually stick to.

Questions everyone asks

What if I miss a session?

Do the next workout in the order and shift the week by a day. If you regularly only make it three times, switch to the 3-day full-body plan.

Where does cardio fit?

Two easy sessions on rest days, or 15–20 minutes after a workout, are plenty. See our cardio plans.

How long should I run this plan?

As long as you keep adding reps or weight. For most people that's many months. When progress stalls, read how to build a workout program to adjust volume and exercises.
