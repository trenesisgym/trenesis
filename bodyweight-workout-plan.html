Bodyweight workout plan: 3 levels, no equipment

A no-equipment home plan with three levels for every movement, from complete beginner to advanced, and a clear rule for when to move up.

By Volodymyr and Linus3 min readUpdated October 2026

Key takeaways

  • Train 3 days a week, with 3 sets of each movement, stopping 1–3 reps before failure.
  • Each movement has 3 levels; move up one movement at a time when you hit the top of the rep range.
  • Pulling is the hardest without equipment, so a doorway pull-up bar is the one thing worth buying.

Bodyweight training works, as long as it keeps getting harder. Without weights, you make it harder by moving to a tougher version of each exercise. This plan gives you three levels for every movement and a simple rule for when to move up.

How the plan works

  • Train 3 days a week on non-consecutive days, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday.
  • Do every movement in the table, 3 sets each, resting 1–2 minutes between sets.
  • Stop each set when you could do about 1–3 more good reps.
  • Everyone starts at the level that fits them. You can be on level 2 for squats and level 1 for push-ups.

The movements and levels

Movement Level 1 Level 2 Level 3
Squat (8–15 reps, per leg on levels 2–3) Squat to a chair Split squat Bulgarian split squat (back foot on a chair)
Push (6–15 reps) Push-ups with hands on a table or bench Push-ups on the floor Push-ups with feet on a chair
Hinge (10–20 reps, per leg on levels 2–3) Glute bridge Single-leg glute bridge Single-leg hip thrust (shoulders on a sofa)
Pull (6–12 reps) Doorway towel row Row under a sturdy table Pull-ups or slow pull-up negatives (needs a bar)
Shoulders (6–12 reps) Pike push-up, hands on a chair Pike push-up, hands on the floor Pike push-up, feet on a chair
Core (20–60 seconds, or 8–12 reps per side) Dead bug Plank Side plank, then hollow body hold

Only use a door, towel or table for rows if it can safely hold your full weight. Test it carefully before you lean back.

When to move up a level

Once you can do the top of the rep range on all 3 sets with good form, move to the next level for that movement only. Your reps will drop; build them back up.

Some movements take a few weeks per level, others a few months. That's normal: progress is still progress.

Ways to make any level harder

If you're stuck between levels, use one of these first:

  1. Slow the lowering part to 3–4 seconds.
  2. Pause for 2 seconds at the bottom.
  3. Add "one and a half" reps: go all the way down, half way up, back down, then all the way up. That counts as one.

Key insight

Pulling is the weak spot of no-equipment training. If you buy one thing, make it a doorway pull-up bar. It covers rows, pull-ups and hanging knee raises.

After level 3

You can keep going with harder calisthenics, such as archer push-ups and pistol squats, or add a pair of dumbbells and switch to our dumbbell plan.
