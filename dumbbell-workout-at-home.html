Dumbbell workout at home: a 3-day full-body plan

A complete beginner plan for home with just a pair of dumbbells: two full-body workouts, three days a week, with sets, reps and what to do when the dumbbells get too light.

By Volodymyr and Linus3 min readUpdated November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Two full-body dumbbell workouts, three days a week, with a rest day between sessions.
  • A bench is useful but optional: every exercise has a floor version.
  • When your dumbbells feel too light, slow down, pause or train one side at a time before buying heavier ones.

A pair of dumbbells is enough to train every muscle at home. This plan works like our gym plan for beginners: two workouts, alternated over three days a week.

What you need

  • A pair of adjustable dumbbells, or two or three pairs of fixed ones. A range of roughly 5–20 kg (10–45 lb) per dumbbell covers most beginners for months.
  • Optional: a flat or adjustable bench. Without one, use the floor versions below.
  • About 2 × 2 m (6 × 6 ft) of floor space.

Choose adjustable or fixed dumbbells for this plan. If you add a bench, an adjustable one covers both the flat presses and incline rows; otherwise use the no-bench swaps.

The week

Week Monday Wednesday Friday
Odd weeks Workout A Workout B Workout A
Even weeks Workout B Workout A Workout B

Any three days work, as long as there's a rest day in between. Each workout takes about 40–50 minutes.

Workout A

Exercise Sets × reps Rest No bench?
Goblet squat 3 × 8–12 2 min No change needed
Dumbbell bench press 3 × 8–12 2 min Floor press
One-arm dumbbell row 3 × 8–12 per arm 2 min Support your hand on a chair
Romanian deadlift 3 × 8–12 2 min No change needed
Lateral raises 2 × 10–15 1–2 min No change needed
Dead bug 2 × 8–12 per side 1 min No change needed

Workout B

Exercise Sets × reps Rest No bench?
Reverse lunges 3 × 8–12 per leg 2 min No change needed
Seated dumbbell shoulder press 3 × 8–12 2 min Standing shoulder press
Chest-supported row (bench set to an incline) 3 × 8–12 2 min Bent-over row

Overhead triceps extension 2 × 10–15 1 min No change needed

Stop each set when you could do about 1–2 more good reps, and do one or two lighter warm-up sets before the first exercise.

Cool-down (5 minutes)

After your last exercise, walk or cycle easily for 2–3 minutes, then do 3–4 gentle stretches for the muscles you trained, 20–30 seconds each. See the 5-minute cool-down routine for which stretches go with which day.

How to progress

Use the same double-progression rule as in the gym: once you reach the top of the rep range on every set, move to the next weight and build the reps back up.

When the dumbbells get too light

Every home trainee hits this point. Before you buy heavier dumbbells, make the same weight harder:

  1. Go higher in reps. Anything up to about 20–30 reps still builds muscle if you finish close to failure.
  2. Slow down. Take 3 seconds on the way down.
  3. Pause for 1–2 seconds at the hardest point, for example the bottom of a squat.
  4. Train one side at a time. Bulgarian split squats instead of goblet squats, single-leg hip thrusts instead of two-leg ones.

Key insight

Getting the dumbbells into position safely matters as much as the exercise. For floor and bench presses, start seated with the dumbbells on your thighs and roll back with them.

Run this plan for 8–12 weeks, then move on to our 4-day home plan. Can you train four days a week already? You can start there now; it has a gentler start for beginners.
