After about six months of consistent training, three full-body sessions a week can start to feel limiting. This plan splits your week into upper-body and lower-body days, so each muscle gets more work and more recovery.

## Who it's for

- You've trained regularly for **6 months or more** and know the basic exercises.
- You can train **4 days a week**.
- You have **adjustable dumbbells** (ideally up to 24 kg / 50 lb or more each), an **adjustable bench** and a **pull-up bar**. No pull-up bar? Swap pull-ups for one-arm rows.

## The week

| Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | Thursday | Friday | Weekend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upper A | Lower A | Rest | Upper B | Lower B | Rest or easy cardio |

## Upper A

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell bench press | 3 × 6–10 | 2–3 min |
| Pull-ups (or band-assisted) | 3 × 6–10 | 2–3 min |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 × 8–12 | 2 min |
| Chest-supported dumbbell row | 3 × 8–12 | 2 min |
| Lateral raises | 3 × 12–15 | 1–2 min |
| Dumbbell curls | 2 × 10–15 | 1 min |

## Lower A

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 8–12 per leg | 2–3 min |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8–12 | 2–3 min |
| Goblet squat | 2 × 10–15 | 2 min |
| Single-leg calf raise | 3 × 10–15 per leg | 1 min |
| Hanging knee raises (or dead bugs) | 3 × 8–15 | 1 min |

## Upper B

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 3 × 6–10 | 2–3 min |
| One-arm dumbbell row | 3 × 8–12 per arm | 2 min |
| Feet-raised push-ups | 3 × 8–15 | 2 min |
| Chin-ups (or band-assisted) | 3 × 6–10 | 2 min |
| Rear-delt flyes | 2 × 12–15 | 1 min |
| Overhead triceps extension | 2 × 10–15 | 1 min |

## Lower B

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell hip thrusts | 3 × 8–12 | 2–3 min |
| Reverse lunges | 3 × 8–12 per leg | 2 min |
| Single-leg Romanian deadlift | 2 × 8–12 per leg | 2 min |
| Sliding leg curls (feet on a towel) | 2 × 8–12 | 1–2 min |
| Side plank | 2 × 30–45 s per side | 1 min |

## How hard and how to progress

- Take most sets to **0–2 reps from failure**. You've learned the movements; now effort drives progress.
- Use **double progression**: reach the top of the rep range on every set, then go up in weight.
- **Track every session.** At this stage progress is slower, and your log is the only way to see it.
- If you feel run down for a couple of weeks, do one lighter week with fewer sets, then carry on.

## When you run out of weight

Heavy lower-body work is the hardest part of training at home. Before buying heavier dumbbells:

1. Use **single-leg** versions: Bulgarian split squats, single-leg hip thrusts and single-leg Romanian deadlifts double the load per leg.
2. **Slow the lowering part** to 3 seconds and **pause** at the bottom.
3. Work in **higher rep ranges** (up to about 20–30), as long as sets end close to failure.

> **Key insight**
> At this stage, sleep, protein and consistency matter more than finding a better exercise. If progress stalls, check those first; our [recovery guide](recovery-and-sleep.html) explains how.
