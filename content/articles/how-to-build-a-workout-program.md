Every program, however fancy, is built from the same few variables. Understand them and you can judge any program, or adjust your own when life gets in the way.

## The key training variables

| Variable | What it means | Good starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Times you train a muscle per week | 2–3 times |
| Volume | Hard sets per muscle per week | 10–15 sets |
| Effort | How close to failure you take a set | 0–2 reps short of failure |
| Rep range | Reps per set | 5–12 |
| Rest | Time between sets | At least 2 minutes |

### Reps: less important than you think

Anything from about 5 to 30 reps per set builds a similar amount of muscle, as long as the set ends close to failure. Lower reps tend to cause less fatigue, so a practical approach is to use the fewest reps you feel comfortable and safe with on each exercise.

### The progression rule

Pick a target, for example 12 reps. Once you hit it with a weight, increase the weight, build the reps back up and repeat. Simple, and it works for years.

## Three splits that work

Pick the one that fits your schedule, then stick with it for at least 8–12 weeks.

### Option A: Full body (2–3 days a week)

Every session trains all major muscle groups, with rest days in between. Ideal if you can only get to the gym two or three times a week.

**How to set it up:** choose one exercise per muscle group, use the same selection each session and do 2–3 sets of each.

### Option B: Upper/lower (4 days a week)

Two upper-body days (chest, back, shoulders, arms) and two lower-body days (quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves). Sessions stay shorter while each muscle still gets enough work and recovery.

**How to set it up:** on upper days, pick one exercise each for chest, back, shoulders, biceps and triceps. On lower days, one each for quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves. Do 2–3 sets of each and alternate upper and lower days.

### Option C: Torso/limbs (4 days a week)

Two torso days (chest, back, shoulders, core) and two limb days (arms and legs). Useful if you want to prioritise your arms: train them first on limb days, when you're fresh.

**How to set it up:** on torso days, one exercise each for chest, back, shoulders and core. On limb days, one each for biceps, triceps, quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves. Do 2–3 sets of each and alternate.

> **Pick and commit**
> All three work. The best split is the one you can show up for every week. Program hopping every few weeks is one of the [most common beginner mistakes](strength-training-mistakes.html).
