How hard you go matters more than which machine you use. Heart rate zones are a simple way to make sure easy days are easy and hard days are actually hard.

## Estimate your maximum heart rate

The classic formula is **220 minus your age**. For a 20-year-old that's 200 beats per minute. It's only an estimate, and can easily be 10 or more beats off for an individual, so use it as a starting point and adjust using how sessions feel. A chest-strap heart rate monitor makes zone training much easier.

## The five zones

| Zone | % of max HR | How it feels | What it trains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 1 | 50–60% | Very easy | Active recovery |
| Zone 2 | 60–70% | You can hold a conversation | Aerobic base, fat use |
| Zone 3 | 70–80% | Comfortably hard | Aerobic threshold |
| Zone 4 | 80–90% | Hard, breathing heavily | Lactate threshold |
| Zone 5 | 90–100% | Maximum effort | VO2 max, top-end capacity |

## The 80/20 rule

Studies of endurance athletes keep finding the same pattern: about 80% of training at low intensity (Zones 1–2) and 20% at high intensity (Zones 4–5).

The most common mistake is spending most sessions in Zone 3, the "grey zone". It's too hard to recover from properly, but not hard enough to push your top-end fitness.

> **Key insight**
> Zone 2 builds the base that makes every other zone possible. If you can't hold a conversation on an easy run, you're going too hard. Slow down: the adaptations still happen.

## The talk test

No heart rate monitor? Use your breathing:

- **Zone 2:** you can speak in full sentences.
- **Intervals (Zones 4–5):** you can't say more than a few words.
