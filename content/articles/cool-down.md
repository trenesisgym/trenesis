After your last set it's tempting to grab your bag and leave. Five minutes of cool-down lets your heart rate settle, gives you a calm moment to stretch, and makes it easier to switch from gym mode back to the rest of your day.

## What a cool-down does, and what it doesn't

The research is clearer than most gym advice. A large review of cool-down studies found ([Van Hooren & Peake, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-018-0916-2)):

| A cool-down helps with | A cool-down doesn't really help with |
| --- | --- |
| Bringing heart rate and breathing back to normal faster | Muscle soreness the next day |
| Clearing lactate from your blood | Your performance in the next workout |
| Possibly your immune system's recovery after hard sessions | Preventing injuries |

So think of it as a good habit and a moment to stretch, not as the key to recovery. Your recovery mainly comes from [sleep, food and rest days](recovery-and-sleep.html).

## The 5-minute routine

1. **2–3 minutes of easy movement.** Walk on the treadmill, cycle gently or walk around the gym, until your breathing is back to normal.
2. **3–4 stretches for the muscles you trained.** Hold each for 20–30 seconds per side. Stretch to a gentle pull, never to pain, and keep breathing.
3. **Optional: 1 minute of slow breathing.** Breathe in through your nose for about 4 seconds and out for about 6. It helps you calm down after a hard session.

### Which stretches after which workout

| Workout | Stretches |
| --- | --- |
| Upper body | Doorway chest stretch, kneeling lat stretch (hands on a bench), cross-body shoulder stretch, overhead triceps stretch |
| Lower body | Kneeling hip flexor stretch, standing quad stretch, hamstring stretch (heel on a low bench), calf stretch against a wall |
| Full body | Kneeling hip flexor stretch, hamstring stretch, doorway chest stretch, kneeling lat stretch |
| Home or bodyweight | Same as full body: a doorframe and the floor are all you need |

## Want to become more flexible?

Stretching does improve flexibility, but only if you do it regularly. If that's your goal, stretch the tight areas for 5–10 minutes on most days. Right after training is a convenient time, but any time works, for example in the evening.

## Questions everyone asks

### Should I stretch before lifting instead?

Before lifting, a short warm-up works better: 5 minutes of easy cardio and one or two lighter sets of your first exercise. Short stretches are fine too. Avoid long holds of a minute or more right before heavy sets, because they can make you slightly weaker for a while.

### Is foam rolling a good cool-down?

If it feels good, yes. It can make you feel less stiff for a short while, but like a cool-down, it's a comfort tool rather than a recovery boost.

### I'm short on time. Can I skip it?

Yes. Skipping the cool-down won't ruin your progress. If you only have one minute, walk slowly while your breathing settles before you head out.
