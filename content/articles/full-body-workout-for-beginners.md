If you're new to the gym, a full-body plan three times a week is the simplest way to start. You train every muscle often enough to learn the movements, and you never wonder what to do next.

## The week

Train on three non-consecutive days and alternate between two workouts:

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Odd weeks | Workout A | Workout B | Workout A |
| Even weeks | Workout B | Workout A | Workout B |

Any three days work, as long as there's a rest day between sessions. Each workout takes about 45–60 minutes.

## Workout A

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest | At home instead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leg press or squat | 3 × 8–12 | 2–3 min | Goblet squat with a dumbbell |
| Machine chest press or bench press | 3 × 8–12 | 2–3 min | Push-ups (hands raised if needed) |
| Lat pulldown | 3 × 8–12 | 2 min | One-arm dumbbell row |
| Lateral raises | 2 × 10–15 | 1–2 min | Dumbbell lateral raises |
| Leg curl | 2 × 10–15 | 1–2 min | Dumbbell Romanian deadlift |
| Crunches | 2 × 10–15 | 1–2 min | Floor crunches |

## Workout B

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest | At home instead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hip thrusts | 3 × 8–12 | 2–3 min | Glute bridges with a dumbbell |
| Shoulder press | 3 × 8–12 | 2–3 min | Seated dumbbell shoulder press |
| Seated cable row | 3 × 8–12 | 2 min | Chest-supported dumbbell row |
| Leg extension | 2 × 10–15 | 1–2 min | Split squats |
| Dumbbell curls | 2 × 10–15 | 1–2 min | Dumbbell curls |
| Triceps pushdown | 2 × 10–15 | 1–2 min | Overhead dumbbell extension |

## How hard each set should be

Stop each set when you could do about 1–2 more good reps. In the first two weeks, stay a bit further from that point while you learn the movements. Always do 1–2 lighter warm-up sets before your first exercise.

## Cool-down (5 minutes)

After your last exercise, walk or cycle easily for 2–3 minutes, then do 3–4 gentle stretches for the muscles you trained, 20–30 seconds each. See the [5-minute cool-down routine](cool-down.html) for which stretches go with which day.

## How to progress: the double progression rule

1. Pick a weight you can lift for the bottom of the rep range, for example 8 reps.
2. Each session, try to add a rep or two with the same weight.
3. Once you hit the top of the range (12 reps) on every set, add the smallest weight step available.
4. Your reps drop back to around 8. Build them up again and repeat.

Write down every set. That log is how you know the plan is working.

> **Key insight**
> Run this plan for at least 8–12 weeks. When progress slows down and you can train four days a week, move to the [4-day upper/lower plan](upper-lower-workout-plan.html). Already able to train four days a week from the start? That plan has a gentler start for beginners too.

## Questions everyone asks

### What if I miss a session?

Just do the next workout in the order. One missed day changes nothing; missed months do.

### Should I do cardio too?

Yes, it helps your health and recovery. Two or three easy sessions a week on rest days are plenty. See our [cardio plans](cardio-plans.html).

### Do I need supplements for this plan?

No. Eat enough protein and calories for your goal; the [calculator](calculator.html) gives you a starting point.
