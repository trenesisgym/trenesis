Many lifters think of cardio as something you do to lose weight, or as something that kills gains. Done right, it does neither. It makes you healthier, recover faster and handle more work in the gym.

## What cardio does for you

- **Heart health.** Lowers resting heart rate and blood pressure over time.
- **VO2 max.** Your body gets better at delivering oxygen to your muscles, so you get fitter at everything.
- **Fat use.** A trained aerobic system is better at using fat as fuel during exercise.
- **Mental health.** Regular cardio is linked to fewer symptoms of depression and anxiety.
- **Metabolic health.** Improves insulin sensitivity and blood-sugar control.
- **Longevity.** VO2 max is one of the strongest predictors of a long, healthy life.

> **Key insight**
> In a large meta-analysis, each 1-MET increase in fitness (about 3.5 ml/kg/min of VO2 max) was linked to roughly 13% lower all-cause mortality. A beginner can gain that in 8–12 weeks of consistent training.

## The five types of cardio

### Steady state (Zone 2)

A steady, moderate pace for 20–60 minutes, easy enough that you could hold a full conversation. It's the foundation of all cardio training and the most researched type. Running, cycling, swimming, brisk walking, rowing and the cross trainer all count.

### High-intensity intervals (HIIT)

Short, near-maximal efforts with easy recovery in between, for example 6 × 2 minutes hard with 2 minutes easy. Very time-efficient, but demanding: limit it to one or two sessions a week.

### Tempo or threshold training

"Comfortably hard": breathing heavily but sustainable for 20–40 minutes. It raises the pace you can hold for longer. A classic session is 4 × 4 minutes hard with 3 minutes easy.

### Long slow distance

A longer Zone 2 session of 45–90 minutes. It builds your aerobic base and mental resilience; one a week is a cornerstone of most endurance plans.

### Active recovery (Zone 1)

Easy walking, light cycling or gentle swimming. Not really a workout: it gets blood flowing and helps you recover. 20–40 minutes on rest days is plenty.

> **Pick what you'll keep doing**
> Running, cycling, rowing and swimming all build similar fitness. Choose what you enjoy. Only specialise if you want to get better at one sport, for example run if you want to run faster.
