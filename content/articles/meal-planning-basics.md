Meal planning means deciding in advance, roughly, what you'll eat during the day. That's it. When you don't plan, hunger and convenience make your food choices. When you plan, even loosely, your goals do.

## What meal planning is not

- A strict diet you have to follow perfectly.
- Weighing and tracking every gram of food.
- Something that only works if you prep every meal on Sunday.
- A system that breaks when you skip a day or eat out.

> **Key insight**
> A rough plan beats no plan. Just knowing what you'll have for breakfast and roughly what's for dinner already makes a difference.

## The two numbers that matter

Meal planning comes down to two things: **hitting your calorie target** and **getting enough protein**. Meal timing, variety and exact macros are secondary. Work out your numbers with our guides to [calories and TDEE](calories-and-tdee.html) and [protein](how-much-protein.html), then plan your day around them.

| Goal | Daily strategy |
| --- | --- |
| Fat loss | Eat below your TDEE; keep protein high to protect muscle. |
| Maintenance | Eat roughly at your TDEE; focus on protein and food quality. |
| Muscle gain | Eat slightly above your TDEE (+200–300 kcal); prioritise protein and consistency. |

### Protein first, always

Build each meal around a protein source, then add carbs and fats. This matters most in a calorie deficit, where enough protein protects your muscle.

### How many meals?

There's no magic number. Most people do well with 3–4 meals a day. Spread your protein reasonably evenly instead of eating almost none at breakfast and a huge amount at dinner.

## Meal prep: optional but helpful

You don't need to prep. But if you often grab fast food when you're tired or busy, having food ready removes the temptation at exactly the right moment.

Even prepping just your lunches for the week, in one hour, removes the moment most plans fall apart.

1. Pick one time a week (Sunday works for most people) for a 60–90 minute cook.
2. Cook a big batch of a protein (chicken, minced beef, tofu, boiled eggs), a grain (rice, pasta, oats) and some roasted vegetables.
3. Portion it into containers and refrigerate.
4. Mix and match through the week: same ingredients, different sauces.
5. Cooked protein keeps 3–4 days in the fridge; freeze anything beyond that.

## Questions everyone asks

### Do I need a strict meal plan?

No. A loose daily structure is enough. Rigid plans are fragile; flexible plans stick. Aim for 80% consistency, not perfection.

### How do I handle eating out?

Plan for it. If you're eating out on Friday, keep your other meals that day lighter. Go for protein-rich options and watch portions. One meal out rarely derails progress; the overall pattern matters.

### Is a cheat day fine?

An occasional bigger meal or day is normal and won't ruin your progress. It's how often and how far over your target you go that counts. Frequent huge "cheat days" can easily cancel out a week's deficit.
