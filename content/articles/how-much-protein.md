Get protein right and most other things fall into place. It's the one nutrient that directly supplies the building blocks for new muscle.

## Why protein is different

The amino acids in protein repair the muscle fibres you damage in training and build new muscle. They also support your immune system, enzymes, hormones and connective tissue.

Unlike carbs and fat, your body has no real protein store. That's why a steady intake every day matters more than the occasional high-protein day.

## How much do you need?

The commonly quoted 0.8 g per kg of bodyweight is a minimum to avoid deficiency, not a target for building muscle. For people who train, it's usually too low.

**Aim for about 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight per day.**

| Bodyweight | Daily protein target |
| --- | --- |
| 60 kg | about 95–130 g |
| 70 kg | about 110–155 g |
| 80 kg | about 130–175 g |
| 90 kg | about 145–200 g |

The lower end of the range already gets you most of the benefit; going higher is useful in a calorie deficit, where protein also helps you keep muscle.

> **Myth: high protein damages your kidneys**
> In people with healthy kidneys, higher protein intakes haven't been shown to cause damage. The real downside of very high protein is that it can crowd out carbs and fats, which you also need. If you have kidney disease, follow your doctor's advice.

## Does timing matter?

The "anabolic window" (the idea that you must down a shake within 30 minutes of training) isn't well supported. Your total daily intake matters far more. Spreading protein over 3–5 meals is still a sensible way to hit your target and support recovery.

> **Key insight**
> Protein is the most filling macronutrient, and your body uses about 20–30% of its calories just to digest it. Both make a calorie deficit easier to stick to.
