Most people who stall in the gym make the same handful of mistakes. Fix these and you're ahead of most of the gym.

## The mistakes

### 1. Not tracking your training

If you don't write it down, you won't know whether you're improving. Log every set, rep and weight, in a notes app or a notebook. Progress is invisible day to day; the log shows the trend.

### 2. Ego lifting

Using more weight than you can control takes tension off the target muscle, raises injury risk and trains your ego instead of your body. A controlled 80 kg squat beats a sloppy 120 kg quarter squat.

### 3. Program hopping

Switching programs every 2–3 weeks is one of the biggest mistakes in the gym. You need time to learn movements and build on them. Pick a program and run it for at least 8–12 weeks.

### 4. Too much pushing, not enough pulling

Most people overdo chest and shoulder pressing and undertrain rows and pulldowns. Over time that creates imbalances. As a rule of thumb, match every pushing set with a pulling set.

### 5. Skipping your warm-up sets

A proper warm-up means 2–3 lighter sets of your first exercise before your working sets. It primes your nervous system and joints. Five minutes on the treadmill is not a warm-up.

### 6. Skipping cardio

Cardio isn't just for fat loss: it improves heart health, recovery and how much work you can handle in the gym. See [why cardio matters for lifters](cardio-for-lifters.html).

## Questions everyone asks

### How long until I see results?

Strength improves within 2–4 weeks, mostly because your nervous system gets better at the movements. Visible muscle changes usually take 8–12 weeks of consistent training and eating well. Changes that other people notice often take 3–6 months.

### Should I train when I'm sore?

Light soreness is fine to train through. If it's bad enough to change your technique, wait another day. Most people do best leaving about 48 hours before training the same muscle hard again.

### Can I build muscle while losing fat?

Yes, especially as a beginner, when returning after a break, or if you have a fair amount of fat to lose. For advanced lifters it's very slow.

### Free weights or machines?

Both build muscle. Free weights are more versatile; machines are more stable, which helps beginners focus on the muscle. Choose what you enjoy and can progress on.

### How do I know if I'm training hard enough?

Use reps in reserve (RIR): how many more reps you could do before failing. Aim for 0–2 RIR on most sets. Going to failure is fine, especially on the last set of an exercise.

### Is it bad if I'm not sore after a workout?

No. Soreness is a poor sign of progress. What matters is the stimulus: hard sets, close to failure, with more weight or reps over time. Not being sore doesn't mean the session didn't work.
