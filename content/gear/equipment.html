Dumbbells

Consider for
A flexible home setup

Adjustable dumbbells offer several weights in one footprint. Fixed dumbbells make weight changes simple if you have the storage space.

What to look for

Check the maximum weight, size of each weight increment, grip dimensions and adjustment mechanism. Confirm whether the listing is for one dumbbell or a pair.

The trade-off

Adjustable sets take time to change. Fixed pairs take more room as your collection grows.

Benches

Consider for
More options for a dumbbell setup

A flat bench keeps things simple. An adjustable bench is worth considering if incline exercises are part of your routine.

What to look for

Check stability, pad dimensions, adjustment locks and the manufacturer’s load rating, including body weight and the weight you lift. Measure the footprint before buying.

The trade-off

More adjustment positions do not automatically mean a better bench. A folding design can save storage space but still needs a stable setup.

Barbells & plates

Consider for
A dedicated strength-training space

A barbell setup makes sense when you have room for the bar, plates and the supporting equipment your exercises require.

What to look for

Match the bar’s sleeve diameter to the plates and collars. Check bar length, load rating, floor protection and space around the lifting area.

The trade-off

The bar is only one part of the cost. Account for plates, storage and a suitable rack with safeties where needed.

Kettlebells

Consider for
A compact, specific addition

Consider a kettlebell when your routine includes kettlebell movements, rather than buying one simply to add variety.

What to look for

Look at handle clearance, finish, base stability and the weights your planned exercises call for.

The trade-off

One weight may suit some movements and be too light or too heavy for others.

Pull-up bars & dip stations

Consider for
Bodyweight training at home

Choose equipment that fits both your space and the movements you intend to practice.

What to look for

Follow the manufacturer’s mounting requirements. Check doorway or wall compatibility, fixings, clearance and load limits before purchase and installation.

The trade-off

Doorway bars are not universally compatible. Freestanding stations need enough floor space and a stable surface.
